# Editing guardrails

Use these rules when turning a raw-to-rough-cut comparison into a decision card.

## Authority boundary

- Never edit audio or mark a segment for automatic deletion.
- Never claim that a cut will maximize traffic, retention, or completion.
- Treat performance differences as historical context and testable hypotheses.
- Give the hosts' written editorial intent priority over inferred historical patterns.

## Required evidence for every candidate

Include all of the following:

1. Raw timestamp and an excerpt.
2. The segment's likely function in the episode.
3. A reason someone might shorten it.
4. A reason someone might keep it.
5. The evidence source: current transcript, declared intent, or named historical episode.
6. Risk level and confidence.
7. A blank human decision field: keep, shorten, remove, or listen back.

If a keep rationale cannot be stated, leave the candidate at `listen back`. Do not upgrade it to a removal recommendation.

## Risk levels

### Green: low editorial risk

Use only for clear production residue such as device checks, pre-roll coordination, post-roll operations, or an exact repeated take. The tool may mark these high-confidence review candidates but still must not modify media.

### Yellow: contextual review required

Use for repeated explanations, parallel examples, long branches, frequent back-and-forth, or transitions. Always show both possible value and possible cost.

### Red: protected

Use when a segment overlaps a declared must-keep range or protected phrase. Also prefer red for emotional turns, vulnerability, relationship chemistry, context required by later claims, and show-specific digressions. Do not recommend removal. Warn if the rough cut lost the segment.

## Final card structure

Start with the declared episode question, listener takeaway, lead host, desired feeling, and one experiment. Follow with protected material, then a candidate table. End with no more than three observations and one listen-back checklist.

Keep generic heuristic text visibly separate from semantic conclusions. Label missing history, missing speaker data, or low alignment instead of filling gaps.
